How it works, and what it can't see

  • The idea (from Arthur Hayes' writing on liquidity): coins crushed 75–90% can recover when dollar liquidity is rising. Two ways in: an asymmetric sleeve (tiny positions, early, hoping for 3x+) and a confirmed sleeve (after a higher low and a structure break).
  • Rules fixed first. Every threshold is the specification's default (hayes/profile.yaml); none is tuned on these results.
  • No hindsight. Each day uses only prices up to that day and liquidity data from its publication date.
  • What history can't include: past token unlock schedules, fees and developer activity aren't available for free day by day, so those filters (which would remove weak coins) are not in the history test. Perpetual-futures data isn't in it either, so the entry thresholds are 5 points higher, as the specification says.
  • All free, nothing manual: Binance and Coinbase public price data (delisted coins included), the Federal Reserve's FRED data, DefiLlama stablecoin totals.
  • Paper only. No real orders, no wallet, no key that can trade. A halted engine opens nothing, not even on paper.

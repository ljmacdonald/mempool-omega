About the Small coins page

The Exchange coins page scans the 150 most-traded coins on Binance. This page looks at the next tier down: coins trading between about $300,000 and $1M a day. It keeps only those that, over the last 90 days, rose 20% or more within a week at least 15% of the time, and shows the 10 with the best score.

These coins move a lot more, in both directions. Thin trading makes them easier to push around, so the fake-signal checks run on the best 15 before the top 10 are shown, and every card shows how often the coin rose and fell 20%+ in a week. Use smaller amounts here.

How to use this page

  • Pick a speed at the top: Quick means you sell within 1 hour, Short within 4 hours, Day within 24 hours.
  • Pick how often to refresh. Every refresh downloads fresh prices and re-ranks the coins on your device (about 150 on this page). Rankings change when a new price candle closes (every 5, 15 or 60 minutes, depending on the speed).
  • Read the top 5. Each card shows when the idea was suggested and at what price, the live price now and how much it has moved since, where to take profit, where to get out to limit a loss, and the latest time to sell.
  • Watch the live chart. Each card has candles that move in real time. The dashed blue line is the price when the idea was suggested (the baseline), green is the take profit, and red is the safety exit.
  • Use the profit calculator. Type how much you'd put in. It shows how many coins you'd get and what you'd make at the take profit, lose at the safety exit, or make if you sold right away, all after fees. You can also type your own target price.
  • Check the fake-signal scorecard. Each idea is checked for common market tricks before it's shown (see below).
  • If you buy one, tap “I bought this”. The My trades tab then tells you what to do: Hold, Take profit now, Exit now, Time's up: sell now, or Consider leaving early.

What the numbers mean

  • Score (0–10): 5 means roughly break-even after fees. Higher is better. Below 5 is “avoid”.
  • Chance it beats the market: the computer's estimate that this coin does better than the average coin over the same time. 50% means no better than average.
  • Take profit: sell here for a gain. It is set twice as far as the safety exit, so you aim to win $2 for each $1 you risk.
  • Safety exit (stop-loss): sell here to keep a loss small. This is the most important line.
  • Sell by: if neither exit happens by this time, sell anyway.
  • To risk $10, buy …: how much to buy so that hitting the safety exit loses about $10. Decide how much you can lose first.
  • Risk level: how much this coin usually swings for the chosen speed. Higher risk means bigger moves both ways.
  • Market mood: how many coins look positive right now. When it says Unfavourable, doing nothing is a good choice.

Fake-signal checks (market manipulation)

Before an idea is shown, the 10 best candidates are checked with live data, and failed checks push an idea down the list:

  • Spoofing: the order book is read twice, 3 seconds apart. Big orders that vanish immediately are fake walls meant to trick people.
  • Thin order book: if few real orders sit near the price, one large trade can move it.
  • Fake (wash) trading: identical-size trades bouncing buy/sell within seconds mean someone is trading with themselves to fake activity.
  • Other exchanges: the price and daily move are compared with OKX and Gate.io. A move on only one exchange is suspicious.
  • Whales: if recent trades are many times bigger than usual, a few big players are driving the price.
  • Plus warnings for coins that already spiked, look overheated, trade very little, or when Bitcoin is falling.

Staying ahead of manipulators who know they're being watched

This system's rules are public, so we assume clever manipulators have read them and will try to game them: passing every check on purpose, pumping a coin until it tops our list and then selling to followers, or pushing the price through obvious exit levels to trigger everyone's safety exits. The defences:

  • Secret, changing limits: every check's exact threshold is different on your device and changes every hour, so nobody can aim just under it.
  • Three order-book readings at random times: fake orders would have to stay up long enough to risk actually being filled.
  • Real money moves prices: lots of trading that barely moves the price is treated as fake, however it's disguised.
  • “Too good to be true”: a setup built only from cheap-to-fake signals (volume bursts, buy spikes), with no lasting trend, is marked down.
  • Bait monitor: every night the system checks whether its own past suggestions dropped right after being suggested. If a pattern keeps doing that, it's penalised automatically.
  • Safety exits away from crowded levels: your exit isn't placed on round numbers or just under the recent low, where stop-hunters aim.
  • No panic from one reading: “Consider leaving early” only appears if the warning shows up on two refreshes in a row.

No system can stop someone who spends real money to genuinely move a price and then sells. That's what the safety exit and the time limit are for.

Blockchain-level checks (mempool, stablecoin flows, wallet clustering, liquidation cascades, funding games) run in the Bitcoin/Ether practice account, where that data exists. Most other coins have no free data of that kind.

Real costs and where to buy

  • Open “Where to buy it cheapest” on any idea. For the amount you typed, it checks the live order books of 7 exchanges (Binance, OKX, Bitget, Gate.io, HTX, Kraken, Coinbase) and the biggest decentralised exchange pools (Uniswap, PancakeSwap and others).
  • For each, it works out the actual money you'd take out: trading fee on the buy and the sell, slippage (your order eating through the order book), the fee to withdraw your money and, on decentralised exchanges, network gas and the risk of front-running bots (“sandwich attacks” that buy just before you and sell just after).
  • The cheapest place for your amount is highlighted. Small amounts are hit hardest by fixed costs like withdrawal fees and gas, which the table shows.
  • On decentralised exchanges, always check the token's contract address, because fakes copy popular names. Use a wallet or network option with MEV protection so bots can't see your trade before it happens.

Simple rules that protect you

  • Never use money you cannot afford to lose. Many traders risk only 1–2% of their money on one idea.
  • Always respect the safety exit.
  • Don't chase coins that already jumped a lot. The page warns you.
  • No idea is a sure thing. A 60% chance still loses 4 times out of 10.

Honest limits

No computer can reliably predict which coin will rise. Tested on past data, this system's top picks only roughly match an average coin. The Track record tab shows how its live ideas really turn out, so judge it by that. Prices come directly from Binance's public market data; the models are retrained every night.

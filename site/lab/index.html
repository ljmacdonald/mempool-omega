Why this page exists

GitHub has thousands of trading bots, many showing amazing results or very high hit rates. Almost all of those results come from testing the rules on the same prices they were tuned on, from accidentally using prices that weren't known yet, or from leaving out trading costs. Anyone with a strategy that truly works keeps it private. So instead of trusting claims, this page takes the most famous public strategies, copies their rules exactly, and tests them the same strict way as everything else on this site:

  • After trading costs (exchange fees, spreads and slippage for each market).
  • Against random entries: every trade is compared with entering the same market at random times, in the same direction, for as long. A strategy that doesn't beat that adds nothing, even if it made money.
  • After it was published: a rule that only worked before people knew about it is no use now. Where the publication date is known, the later years are judged on their own; otherwise the most recent third.

Tested every night on the longest free history: up to 30 years of daily prices, 2 years of hourly crypto, 180 days of 5-minute crypto, 60 days of 5-minute US funds. Results are per trade, as a % of the money in the trade.

Scoreboard

Hit rate = share of trades that made money after costs. On its own it means little: a strategy that takes small profits quickly and lets losses run can win 9 times out of 10 and still lose money. Look at the average per trade and whether it beat random entries.

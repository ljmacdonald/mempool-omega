What this page does

Every 15 minutes while the market is open, the system checks the 7 major currency pairs, 21 popular crosses, gold and silver. For each one it checks both directions: buying the pair, betting the first currency strengthens, and selling it, betting it weakens. A model trained on recent price history ranks them after costs, and the manipulation checks below mark ideas down or rule them out.

Exotic pairs (naira, rand, shilling, cedi, Egyptian pound, lira, peso, real, rupee) are shown separately, for information only. Their spreads are wide, and several are managed by their central banks, so the official rate can be far from the rate you can actually get.

Manipulation checks

Between 2014 and 2015, Citigroup, JPMorgan, Barclays, RBS, UBS, HSBC and Bank of America paid about $10 billion in fines for rigging currency benchmarks and trading ahead of client orders. The tactics are known, so the page defends against them:

  • Rate-fixing windows: traders pushed prices around the 4 pm London fix. No idea is suggested, and no sell-by time is set, inside the London, ECB (14:15 Frankfurt) or Tokyo (9:55) fix windows. A recent spike-and-reverse at a fix is flagged as a manipulation footprint.
  • Stop hunts: prices are pushed through levels where many stop orders sit, and then snap back. The page detects recent "swept the low and bounced" moves and failed breakouts. Your safety exit is kept off round levels (every 50 pips) and off levels just swept.
  • Off-market prices: every cross pair must agree with the price implied by the two related dollar pairs (for example, EUR/JPY = EUR/USD × USD/JPY). If it doesn't, it's excluded.
  • Thin markets: the Sunday open, the daily 5 pm New York changeover and Friday evening have little trading and wide spreads, which makes them the easiest times to push prices. These get a warning.
  • News ambushes: big announcements move prices 1–2% in seconds and spreads explode. No entry in the 30 minutes before, and a heavy penalty if one falls inside the holding time.
  • Hidden limits: the exact thresholds are secret and change every hour, always in the stricter direction.

Leverage, in plain words

Leverage lets you trade more money than you have. At 10x, $100 controls $1,000, so a 1% move makes or loses $10. If your losses reach about half your money, most brokers close your trade automatically (a "margin call"). At 30x, that happens after a move of under 2% against you. The calculator shows this for every idea. Start with no leverage.

Costs

  • Spread: about 0.01% on major pairs, 0.025% on crosses, 0.04% on gold, and often 0.6% or more on exotics.
  • Overnight fee (swap): charged for holding past 5 pm New York. It's roughly 0.01% a night here, but it depends on your broker and on interest rates.

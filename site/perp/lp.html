How it works, and what it can't do

  • Four strategies, one core. Every trade goes through the same cost model: GMX's own published fees (0.04% or 0.06%), its price-impact formula and settings, the real funding and borrowing rates, the execution fee paid by recent real orders (measured), Arbitrum gas, and for hedges Hyperliquid's documented fees, order-book slippage and funding. Net = gross − every cost, computed in one place, in exact decimal money.
  • Rules fixed first. Thresholds are the two specifications' defaults (perp/config.yaml); nothing is tuned on results, so every history period is out of sample. Results are split by period, market and side.
  • Two accounts. The $100 account (an equal share per strategy) is judged; a $10,000 reference account runs the same rules to show whether an edge exists once fixed costs matter less.
  • Six go/no-go gates (data integrity 99.5%+, 500+ trades, a positive 95% lower bound for net profit, drawdown ≤ 10%, still profitable with costs doubled, 30 days of live paper). Passing all six only allows a review.
  • Risk rules a strategy can't change: 0.5% risk per position including costs, 2% daily loss and 10% drawdown limits (pause and flatten), margin ≤ 20%, leverage ≤ 2x, at most 1% of a market's liquidity, one position per side in the correlated group. Kill switches: a stale oracle (over 5 minutes) or a USDC depeg (over 1%) flattens; GMX and Hyperliquid prices more than 2% apart stop trading.
  • What the free data can't do: act every 10 seconds (the free scheduler runs hourly, so fills use the next hour's prices); historical open-interest balance and pool utilisation (those filters run going forward only); 6-hourly pool re-ranking (daily history).
  • Paper only, for good. No wallet, no key, no signing or sending code exists. As far as we know GMX's app, Hyperliquid and dYdX bar US users, and this project is paper only. Prices and research only: not financial advice.

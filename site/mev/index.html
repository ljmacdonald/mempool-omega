What this lab does

Every hour, for about 8 minutes, it looks for round trips on Solana: buy a token on one exchange with the $100 account's whole value in SOL, sell it straight back on another, and end with more SOL than you started with. Prices come from Jupiter quotes, which already include each pool's fee and the price impact of the trade. The sell price is asked for straight after the buy price (within half a second), so a gap isn't just the market moving between the two.

When a round trip would pay after every cost, it is checked again 1, 3, 10 and 30 seconds later with the same amounts and exchanges. Professional bots act within one Solana slot (about 0.4 seconds), so an opportunity that is gone after a second was never really available to anyone slower.

Shadow mode No wallet, no keys: nothing is ever signed or sent. Landing (does a transaction get into a block in time?) can't be measured without sending one, so it is always shown as not enough evidence.

Who you are up against

  • Competing arbitrage bots. Hundreds of programs watch the same pools and close price gaps within a slot or two. Being second means getting nothing, or a loss.
  • No public waiting room on Solana. Unlike Ethereum, Solana has no conventional public mempool: transactions go to the current block leader, and the leader's software decides the order. Bots compete through speed, priority fees and Jito bundles, not by watching a public queue.
  • Jito bundles and tips. Most leaders run Jito's software, which runs short auctions for ordered, all-or-nothing bundles. A tip is a bid: it buys a better chance of being included, never a guaranteed place.
  • Sandwich attacks. A bot can put a buy just before your trade and a sell just after it, pocketing the difference. A strict minimum output limits the damage; Jito's DontFront marker asks Jito's block engine to keep your transaction first in any bundle, which "may help reduce sandwich attacks but is not guaranteed to do so" (Jito) and does nothing outside Jito.
  • Stale opportunities. A price gap seen a second ago may already be gone; trading on it means paying fees for a transaction that then fails its minimum-output check.
  • Paying for failures. A normal transaction that lands but fails still pays its fees. A Jito bundle that would fail is not included, so it costs nothing, but it only counts if a Jito leader picks it up.

No method protects completely from front-running or other bots, and this page never claims one does.

What this lab can't tell you

  • It runs on free cloud servers that wait about a second between price requests; real bots are hundreds of times faster and sit next to the validators. Opportunities that last under a second are invisible here.
  • A Jupiter quote is not an executed trade: the price can move between a quote and a transaction even with no competitor.
  • Results "per landed attempt" assume the transaction got in. How often it would get in is unknown until real transactions are sent, which needs the owner's explicit approval and a separate build.

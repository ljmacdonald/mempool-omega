The rules it follows

  • Capital first. Staying in cash is always allowed and is the default. It only buys when a DEX idea is graded Moderate or better and is still expected to make money after every cost at the real trade size.
  • No trades while the DEX page's grade check says grades can't be trusted, or while that speed is on probation.
  • At most 25% of the account in one trade, sized so that hitting the safety exit loses at most 2% of the account.
  • At most 2 trades open and 1 new trade an hour. The standard DEX list only.
  • No new trades for the rest of the day after a 5% loss in a day (UTC). Everything pauses if the account falls 20% below its peak, until the owner restarts it.

Why the results are realistic

  • No bots, no head start. It acts on the same hourly list everyone sees. It buys at the price at the end of the hour the list came out, so it pays for any run-up after publication, and the front-running cost is added on top.
  • Every cost, both ways, at the real size: pool fees, price impact, sandwich bots (MEV), front-runners and snipers, token taxes and network fees. Network fees are fixed, so they weigh more on small trades.
  • No stop orders. DEXes don't have them, so it checks once an hour and sells at that hour's price. A crash straight through the safety exit is sold where the price landed, which can be far below the plan.
  • Rug-pulls hurt. If the pool's money is pulled, it sells against what is left in the pool, which can be almost nothing.
Paper only: no wallet, no keys, no real orders. The account, every step and every trade are recorded by the project's hourly DEX job on GitHub. Past results do not predict future results.

What ICT is

ICT ("Inner Circle Trader") is a trading method taught by Michael J. Huddleston. Its idea: big players push prices to where other traders' stop orders sit ("liquidity"), fill their own orders there, then move the price the other way, leaving recognisable footprints. This page turns the core model into exact rules a computer can check, so every setup is found the same way every time, and so the rules can be tested.

The setup this page looks for (a buy; a sell is the mirror image)

  1. Liquidity sweep: the price dips below a level where many sell stops sit (yesterday's low, the Asian session low, "equal lows", or a recent swing low) and quickly closes back above it. This is the "stop hunt" or "Judas swing".
  2. Market structure shift with displacement: after the sweep, a strong candle closes above the last swing high. The trend has turned.
  3. Fair value gap (FVG): the fast move leaves a gap between candle 1's high and candle 3's low. The order goes in the middle of that gap ("consequent encroachment"), waiting for the price to come back.
  4. Stop: just below the sweep low. Target: the nearest untouched buy-side liquidity (yesterday's high, equal highs, a swing high) at least twice the risk away, otherwise exactly twice the risk.

The confluence checklist

  • Higher timeframe agrees: the 1-hour chart's last break of structure was in the same direction.
  • Discount / premium: buying in the lower half of the last 24 hours' range (selling in the upper half).
  • Optimal trade entry (OTE): the gap sits 62–79% of the way back down the move.
  • Order block: the gap overlaps the last opposite candle before the move.
  • Kill zone: the move happened in the London open (2–5 am New York), New York open (7–10 am) or London close (10 am–noon).
  • Silver bullet hour: 3–4 am, 10–11 am or 2–3 pm New York.
  • SMT divergence: a closely related market (ETH for BTC, BTC for other coins, GBP/USD for EUR/USD, Nasdaq for S&P, silver for gold) did not make the same new low.
  • Major liquidity: the level swept was yesterday's low/high, the Asian range, or equal lows/highs, not just any swing.
  • Midnight open: buying below the New York midnight opening price (selling above it).

How to read a setup

  • Waiting: the order isn't filled yet. It's a limit order at the entry price, valid for 4 hours. If the price reaches the target first, the setup is missed and cancelled.
  • Entry reached: the order would have filled; the trade is running to its target or stop, for at most 24 hours.
  • Chance of target first comes from how often past setups of the same kind, with the same number of checklist items, reached the target before the stop. With few past examples it's pulled towards the break-even chance, and the grade says "Unproven".

Honest limits

No independent study has shown that ICT concepts give traders an edge. Read the "How the rules did" tab: it shows exactly how these rules did on recent data, after costs, including when they lost. On crypto, exchange fees are large compared with the small stops of 15-minute setups, which hurts results a lot. Paper trading and education only; never use money you can't afford to lose.

What this page does

Every hour the system looks at the busiest pools on Solana, BNB Chain, Ethereum and Robinhood Chain, throws out anything that could be a scam, ranks the rest with a model trained on DEX price history, and works out what you'd really take out after every cost. Your browser then re-checks the best candidates live, for the amount you typed, every time it refreshes.

The safety rules (a token must pass all of them)

  • At least $500,000 of real money in the pool. Only the SOL, ETH, BNB or stablecoin side counts. Scammers create pools "worth" millions by pairing their token with another token they made.
  • At least 14 days old, and actively traded on at least 10 of the last 14 days. Scammers buy old, quiet tokens and "revive" them so they look established.
  • Real people can sell. A simulated buy-and-sell must work (Ethereum and BNB Chain), and at least 120 different wallets must have actually sold in the last 24 hours (180 where no simulation is possible; half that on Ethereum, where every trade costs real network fees). A contract can let a checker sell while blocking ordinary buyers, but it can't fake hundreds of real sellers.
  • Taxes of 5% or less, and nobody can raise them.
  • No dangerous owner powers: printing tokens, freezing wallets, blacklists, pausing trading, taking tokens out of wallets, hidden owners, or code that can be swapped after our check.
  • Not concentrated: the 10 biggest ordinary wallets may hold at most 50%, and the creator at most 20%.
  • The pool's money isn't being pulled (more than a 20% drop in 6 hours rejects the token) or pumped in just before (more than 4x in 3 days).
  • No copies of another token's name or of a major coin, no creator with past scam tokens, and no security settings changed in the last 3 days.

Standard or Higher risk

The Higher risk switch lets in younger and smaller tokens: at least $100,000 in the pool, at least 5 days old, trading on at least 3 days, 100 buyers and 60 sellers a day, and the 10 biggest wallets holding up to 60%. Every scam test stays exactly as strict: sell test, real sellers, taxes, owner powers, liquidity being pulled, copies and rule changes. Younger and smaller tokens still collapse far more often, and the model learned mostly from older tokens, so treat these as long shots and use small amounts. They have their own track record.

Thinking like the scammer

These rules are public, so we assume a skilled scammer has read them and will build a token that passes them, then use the "checked" label to attract buyers. The defences against that:

  • What they could do counts, not what they do today. A tax that can be raised, a blacklist or a pause button is rejected while someone still controls it, even if it's switched off now.
  • Real behaviour beats tests. Many different real wallets selling is required, because tests can be fooled and real selling is expensive to fake.
  • The worst answer wins. Several independent checkers are used (GoPlus, honeypot.is, RugCheck). One red flag from any of them is enough. If a checker can't run, the token isn't suggested.
  • Secret limits that only get stricter. Every limit is tightened by a private amount that changes every hour on your device. The numbers above are the minimum, so nobody can build a token that sits just inside them.
  • Cheap signals are distrusted. Paid promotion on DexScreener, lots of trades from few wallets, volume many times the pool size, and pool money that appeared suddenly all lower the score.
  • Live re-checks. Your browser re-runs the security checks and watches the pool's money every refresh, and every 2 minutes for trades you hold. If anything turns bad, you're told to exit.
  • Bait monitor. Every night the system checks whether its own past ideas dropped right after being suggested, and penalises patterns that keep doing that.

Real costs: what you actually take out

  • Pool fee on the buy and the sell (0.01% to 1%, depending on the pool).
  • Price impact: your own trade moves the price against you. Bigger amounts in smaller pools pay more.
  • Token tax on buying and selling, if any.
  • Front-running bots (MEV "sandwiches"): a bot sees your swap, buys just before you and sells just after. The table shows the cost with and without protection, and how to protect yourself on that network.
  • Snipers: bots and fast traders who buy published ideas before you can. Their effect is measured from how our own past ideas moved right after publication, and charged as an extra entry cost.
  • Network (gas) fees for both swaps.

The break-even price is where you'd get back exactly what you put in after all of that. The score already includes these costs for your amount, so tokens with high costs rank lower.

Honest limits

  • No check can stop someone with real money who builds a clean token, lets it run, and sells into buyers. The safety exit and time limit are your protection.
  • Modern pools can't be locked, so the pool's money can be withdrawn at any time. The app watches for that and tells you to exit, but a very fast pull can happen between checks.
  • Robinhood Chain is new: most of its tokens are younger than 14 days or hold less than $500,000, so often none pass.
  • Ranking updates once an hour (when a new hourly candle closes). Prices, pool money, security checks and costs update every refresh.
  • Paper trading and education only. Never use money you can't afford to lose.
